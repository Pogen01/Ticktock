What TickTock does

When you want a better price than the current one you place a limit order (an order that only trades at your price or better) and wait. TickTock forecasts how long that wait is likely to be: a range, the chance of filling within a few days, and the chance it doesn't fill at all within 40 trading days.

We do not predict price direction. We predict the distribution of the waiting time.

How a "fill" is counted

An order is placed at the day's close. A buy at limit L counts as filled on the first later session whose low is at least one cent below L (sell: high one cent above L). Just touching the price is not enough, because other orders were queued there first.

Models

  • History (Kaplan–Meier): past waits for orders of similar distance and traffic.
  • Random-walk theory: textbook first-passage maths using distance and recent volatility.
  • Theory + traffic: the same formula with volatility rescaled by traffic, market stress, calendar and risk features.
  • Weibull survival: a fitted waiting-time curve using every feature, including the company risk factors from the 2024/2025 sheet.

Risk factors as variables

Each company's risk text is mapped to 11 themes (regulatory, trade & geopolitics, supply chain, AI shift, rates & macro, consumer demand, litigation, competition, M&A, commodities, operational). Flags, counts, "macro exposure" and their interaction with market stress feed the models. 2024 text applies to 2024 orders and 2025 text to 2025 and 2026 orders. The sheet has no text before 2024, so earlier orders use the 2024 text as a fixed company profile.

How accuracy is measured

Models are trained on orders placed from Nov 2006 to Dec 2019, tuned on 2020–2021 (including the COVID crash) and scored once on Jan 2022 – Oct 2026, which none of them saw. For each test order we ask four yes/no questions: does it fill within 1, 5, 10 and 20 trading days? A model says "yes" when its probability is 50% or more. Accuracy is the share of those calls that were right. The baseline always gives the most common answer, so it shows how much the model adds. We also report range hit: how often the real wait landed inside the predicted 80% range (it should be about 80%).

Data: 20 years of daily bars (Oct 2006 – Oct 2026) for the 20 largest US companies at the start of 2024. META, TSLA, V and AVGO start at their listing dates. Waits are in trading days.
